2025–současnost

European Parliament

Administrator advising on parliamentary business

At the European Parliament I work in a political-group secretariat on economic, financial-services and budgetary policy. For selected files the work goes well beyond monitoring: I analyse Commission proposals article by article, prepare technical and political positions, draft amendments, develop voting recommendations and assess compromise options. I follow ECON and BUDG procedures from the Commission proposal through amendment deadlines and technical negotiations to committee or plenary votes. The Market Integration and Supervision Package is the clearest example of detailed legislative ownership, including coordination of a large amendment package across three legislative components. Other work covers the digital euro, RRF implementation, the post-2027 MFF, NRPP, securitisation, own resources, EU borrowing and NGEU, Ukraine financing, banking regulation, Banking Union and capital-market integration.

2022–2025

European Commission — DG ECFIN

Economic Analyst

In DG ECFIN I worked as an Economic Analyst in a country-desk environment, organisationally in G1 covering Czechia, Poland and Slovakia, while individual country responsibilities evolved and included substantial work on Slovakia, Malta and Czechia. I prepared official Commission macroeconomic forecasts using national accounts and high-frequency indicators, covering GDP and its components, inflation, labour markets, wages, external balances and public finances. I also organised and participated in forecast missions and worked on the European Semester, the Macroeconomic Imbalance Procedure, fiscal surveillance and the Recovery and Resilience Facility. A major project was the 2023 revision of Slovakia's RRP, where I developed quantitative approaches to reflect construction-cost and inflation shocks. I am explicitly listed by name as a contributor in ten European Economic Forecasts from Summer 2022 through Spring 2025.

2021–2022

European Central Bank

Financial Stability Analyst — Macroprudential Policy

In the ECB's Macroprudential Policy Division I worked on quantitative financial-stability analysis and the relationship between bank capital requirements and credit supply. The principal pandemic project examined whether banks close to regulatory capital constraints reduced new lending and how effective actual capital-requirement relief was in supporting credit supply. I linked granular AnaCredit loan data with supervisory bank time series, liquidity and other financial indicators. Application records describe a warehouse containing around 100 million loan observations and a broader research dataset exceeding 400 GB. I worked with SQL, distributed data processing and econometric analysis in R. I am explicitly named in the acknowledgements of two ECB Working Papers, one of which was later published in the Journal of Money, Credit and Banking.

2019–2021

European Central Bank

Market Operations Analyst — Bond and International Markets

In the ECB's Bond and International Markets Division I worked directly with financial markets and central-bank portfolios. I prepared statistical analysis of market developments for the bond-market team and material for the Executive Board, contributed to investment strategy for the ECB's foreign-exchange fixed-income portfolio and monitored its performance and risks. The role included compilation and dissemination of data on managed portfolios and foreign-exchange interventions, including database architecture, and policy work on foreign exchange and asset-purchase programmes. I worked on the trading floor using Bloomberg, Reuters, EBS and 360T and interacted with banks, investment funds and other central banks. Technical work included Python analysis of more than 100 GB of tick-by-tick FX data and Tableau tools for VaR, portfolio performance, equities, commodities and volatility.

2016–2019

European Central Bank

Research Analyst — Supervisory Banking Statistics

In ECB Supervisory Banking Statistics I worked on the data infrastructure supporting the Single Supervisory Mechanism. Responsibilities included collecting data from national authorities, processing and validating it and disseminating it to the Statistical Data Warehouse and the European Banking Authority. I also analysed how financial and market developments affected banks' balance sheets and portfolios and developed technical infrastructure for capital, liquidity, credit-risk, stress-test and data-quality indicators. I worked with datasets exceeding 400 GB using SAS, SQL and Tableau and automated recurring reporting processes. In one documented example, redesigned code reduced a daily supervisory-report extraction from roughly six hours to two. This role provided the banking-data foundation for my later work in macroprudential policy and financial stability.

2015–2016

ECB / European Systemic Risk Board Secretariat

Trainee — systemic-risk analysis

At the Secretariat of the European Systemic Risk Board, which is staffed and supported by the ECB, I worked on cross-cutting systemic-risk analysis of the European financial system. One major responsibility was the Systemic Risk Survey: collecting assessments from participating authorities, consolidating and visualising the results and helping automate a process that had previously relied heavily on manual Excel work. I also analysed risks in bank portfolios, real estate, insurers and pension funds and prepared material for the ESRB General Board. Application records also describe econometric analysis of non-performing-loan drivers and larger VAR models in MATLAB. The later paper “EU banks after the crisis” explicitly thanks Magdalena Grothe and Nikolas Mayer for discussions and preliminary work. During the ESRB assignment I also provided economic commentary to Hospodářské noviny on the Czech economy.

2014–2015

European Commission — DG ECFIN

Trainee — macroeconomic imbalances

During my DG ECFIN traineeship I worked on macroeconomic imbalances in Sweden and the United Kingdom. Responsibilities included developing econometric models, updating quantitative methodology and providing statistical and analytical support to the principal country-surveillance publications. The strongest identifiable public output is the 2015 Sweden Country Report, whose section on shock transmission uses “a model developed by the European Commission” to estimate the effects of housing-price declines and interest-rate increases. Employment evidence from the same period states that I developed econometric models for this analytical area. The published Swedish estimates show that a 10% decline in house prices could reduce GDP by 1.4%, private consumption by 2.0% and residential investment by 27.1%. I also provided analytical support to the 2015 United Kingdom Country Report.

2013–2016

University of St. Gallen

M.A. HSG in Quantitative Economics and Finance

The M.A. HSG in Quantitative Economics and Finance at the University of St. Gallen combined advanced macroeconomics, microeconomics, mathematics, statistics, Econometrics I and II, microeconometrics, dynamic programming, asset pricing and theory of finance. My master's thesis focused on GDP forecasting for European economies and compared Bayesian VAR models with neural networks using Bayesian regularisation. The research involved optimisation of parameters, variables and model specifications and comparison of forecast performance. The programme provided the quantitative foundation for later econometric and forecasting work at DG ECFIN, the ECB and ESRB. A public graduate list from the 8 October 2016 Master Graduation Day explicitly lists “Mayer — Nikolas — Moravany — Tschechische Republik” among the thirteen graduates of the programme.
